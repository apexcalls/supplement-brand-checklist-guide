A Practical Checklist for Choosing a Supplement Brand

Choosing a supplement brand does not need to take hours, but it does need a method. Run through this checklist before you buy, and you will avoid most of the common mistakes.

One: read the full ingredient list, not just the front of the pack. Look for every active ingredient with its amount per serving, and check whether any ingredient is hidden inside a proprietary blend without an amount. A brand that will not tell you how much of each ingredient you are taking has not earned your money.

Two: compare the dose with the evidence. If a brand cites a study, check the dose used in that study and compare it with the label dose. A studied ingredient at a tenth of the studied dose is not the same product the study tested, whatever the label implies.

Three: check for independent testing. Look for a recent, batch-specific certificate from an accredited laboratory, covering ingredient amounts, heavy metals, and microbes. A single undated certificate, or testing by the brand itself alone, is weaker evidence than a current independent report.

Four: read the rest of the label. Check the serving size, the number of servings per container, and the price per serving — not per bottle. Check the storage instructions and the expiry date. Check for allergens and for ingredients you want to avoid, such as unnecessary sweeteners or colourings.

Five: look for the brand behind the product. A real company has a real address, a contact route, and a history you can find. A brand that exists only as a single product page, with no company information and no other products, is harder to hold accountable if something goes wrong.

Six: check the claims. A trustworthy brand describes what its product does in measured language and avoids promising cures, rapid transformations, or results that sound too good to be true. Extreme claims are a signal to walk away, not a reason to buy.

Seven: consider your own circumstances. Check for interactions with medicines you take, and for warnings that apply to your health conditions. A product that is safe in general can still be wrong for you in particular, and no checklist replaces professional advice where it matters.

If a brand passes all seven checks, it has earned a trial. If it fails two or more, keep looking — there are plenty of brands that pass.
